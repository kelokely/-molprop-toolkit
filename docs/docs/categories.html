How to read the major analysis categories

MolScope categories are designed to support early decision-making rather than replace assays. The main question is always the same: what should this signal change about the next experiment or design round?

In vitro-relevant modules

Use these first for assay planning and early liability review.

  • Med-chem flags and toxicity alerts for artifact and reactivity risk.
  • Metabolism, CYP burden, and hERG heuristics for counterscreen planning.
  • Solubility and permeability signals for absorption and developability triage.

In vivo-relevant modules

Use these when deciding which series deserve deeper PK and efficacy work.

  • PK heuristics for exposure headwinds.
  • Oral filter families for route feasibility.
  • Developability burden for multi-factor prioritization.

CNS MPO and oral filters

CNS MPO is best used as a balancing signal across lipophilicity, polarity, size, hydrogen-bonding, and basicity. Oral filters are not pass-fail truths; they are compact ways to ask whether the project is drifting into a property region that will demand more formulation or higher doses.

Alerts and liability heuristics

Med-chem, toxicity, hERG, CYP, and metabolism outputs are best read as “investigate next” prompts. They identify chemotypes and property combinations that repeatedly create trouble later, so the output should push teams toward orthogonal assays, counterscreens, or targeted chemistry changes.

Solubility, permeability, PK, and developability

These sections help answer whether a compound is merely interesting on paper or plausibly practical enough to advance. They are strongest when used comparatively within a series or against a project’s current leads, not as stand-alone claims about absolute in vivo behavior.

Series and shape context

Scaffold, cluster, and optional 3D-shape outputs become much more useful once a project is comparing analog families rather than single compounds. Use them to understand whether a liability is local to one chemotype or persistent across the campaign.

Operational rule: if a category changes what you would synthesize, test, or deprioritize next, it is doing its job. If it only produces columns and no action, the report or picklist should do more interpretive work.
