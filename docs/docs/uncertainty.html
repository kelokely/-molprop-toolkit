Evidence hierarchy

Confidence should track evidence quality, not interface polish

MolScope now surfaces decision products and campaign-memory outputs. That only helps if the site makes it obvious what is measured, what is heuristic, what is calculated, and what is campaign-derived summary.

Measured Calculated Heuristic Campaign-derived

These evidence types should not be treated as interchangeable. Measured data usually deserves the most weight, campaign-derived summaries depend on the quality of the underlying rows, and heuristic or calculated signals are best used for ranking and question-setting.

Confidence label Typical evidence mix How to act on it
High Measured potency or assay support is present, missingness is low, and the recommendation is consistent with campaign context. Reasonable to use as a primary review signal, while still checking for project-specific exceptions.
Medium Mixed measured and heuristic support, or good calculated support with some missing key assays. Useful for prioritization, but should usually trigger a discriminating assay or rescue design before stronger commitment.
Low Mostly heuristic evidence, sparse analog context, conflicting signals, or major missing measured endpoints. Treat as a prompt for what to test next, not as a reason to over-interpret the current ranking.

What lowers confidence

Missing potency, missing counterscreens, sparse series context, one-off transforms, and recommendations driven mostly by heuristics rather than measured outcomes.

What raises confidence

Measured support, repeated analog trends, consistent round-over-round movement, and recommendations that survive multiple evidence types pointing the same way.

Tentative campaign learnings

A learning is tentative when it comes from one transform, one series branch, or a mostly heuristic table without much measured confirmation.

Robust campaign learnings

A learning becomes stronger when several analogs, rounds, or matched-pair contexts support the same directional conclusion and the measured outcomes agree.
